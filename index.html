I am a graduate student at Show Lab, National University of Singapore, advised by Prof. Mike Zheng Shou. I received my B.Eng. in Automation from Zhejiang University in 2024. I also work as an AI Scientist at Kokoro Lab, Mikomiko (Singapore), where I train and accelerate large-scale image generation models.

My research sits at the intersection of multimodal generation and robot learning: how diffusion and flow-matching models can be aligned, distilled, and steered into policies that act reliably in the physical world.

Lately I have been asking what failure can teach us about success — using near-miss trajectories, failure boundaries, and the geometry that survives video generation as supervision signals. The common thread: the supervision we usually throw away is often the most informative part of the data.

When Failures Outline Success: Contrastive Denoising for Fine-Grained Robotic Manipulation

Zhijun Cao*, et al.

Under review, NeurIPS 2026

In fine-grained manipulation, ~20% of real-robot trajectories fail and are routinely discarded. We show fewer than 3% are truly off-distribution — the rest are near-misses that share the success scaffold and deviate only along a few task-critical dimensions. Training an outcome-conditioned policy with learnable success / failure / null tokens turns those failures into a repulsive signal at inference, a bidirectional generalization of CFG that needs only trajectory-level labels.

Where Success Breaks: Failure-Boundary Learning for Robust Vision-Language-Action Models

Zhijun Cao*, et al.

Under review, CoRL 2026

SFT post-training tells a policy whether it failed, never where it began to drift. We recast robust adaptation as failure-boundary learning — discover boundaries via 128 parallel digital-twin rollouts, localize them with privileged simulator state, then shape the flow-matching velocity field directly, with no action likelihood and no critic. 50 real demonstrations beat SFT trained on twice the data by 17.8 points under unseen conditions.

Reinforcement Learning Flow Matching Sim-real Co-training

Supervise What Survives: Geometry-Guided VLA Adaptation from Synthetic Robot Videos

Zhijun Cao, et al.

Under review, CoRL 2026

Synthesizing training data from human video scales the dataset but hurts performance, because video generation preserves geometry (the where) while destroying control (the how). We let the surviving geometry supervise only vision — routing future 2D end-effector waypoints to the visual backbone as a spatial anchor — and train the action head on real data alone. +14.5 points over pseudo-action baselines at a quarter of the real-robot budget.

Imitation Learning Based Manipulator Skill Training for Home Environment

Zhijun Cao, et al.

Chinese Automation Congress (CAC 2024), EI — Outstanding Undergraduate Thesis, College of Electrical Engineering, ZJU

Diffusion policies spend the same denoising budget on every timestep. We allocate steps dynamically via a coarse-to-fine workspace test: compress aggressively when the end-effector is far from the target, restore full denoising as it closes in. 3.5 steps on average reach 0.80 success — beating a fixed 5-step schedule (0.68) with fewer steps, and cutting inference from 1.05 s to 0.30 s.

Show-Pro: Semantic Harness Enables VLMs to Play Robots 2026.06 – present
Core contributor · co-first-author preprint in preparation
  • Frontier VLMs carry strong world knowledge but no executable action interface. We design a semantic harness that translates their perception–reasoning output into robot actions, enabling zero-shot manipulation with 90%+ success and no training data at all.
  • Their own rollouts are then distilled into an open-source base model as synthetic supervision, retaining 85%+ success across unseen embodiments, tasks, environments and objects at controllable inference cost.
